About Me

The story so far

I began my B.Sc. Agriculture in 2023, my introduction to research came through assisting a senior with a study, which sparked my curiosity about how scientific questions are investigated and answered. Later, my involvement in AICRP forage trials gave me practical exposure to agricultural research, from field observations to data analysis. A biotechnology internship further broadened my perspective, introducing me to the potential of microorganisms in developing sustainable agricultural solutions.

Alongside these experiences, working on a startup project introduced me to the importance of agricultural extension and communicating knowledge in ways that can reach farmers. Together, these experiences have helped me explore different sides of agriculture, from understanding biological processes to considering how research can be applied beyond the laboratory and field. As I approach the completion of my undergraduate degree, I want to continue learning, strengthen my research skills, and explore new questions in agricultural science. In the long term, I hope to build a career that allows me to contribute to research, develop practical solutions, and remain open to where new ideas and experiences may lead.

Fall Armyworm Larval Density Prediction Prototype (Independent Project)

Model Overview

The prototype estimates weekly fall armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) larval density in maize using the input meteorological week, local weather parameters, and moth-trap observations.

Model Development
  • Predictive foundation: Developed using 2023–2024 Kharif-season data from Pantnagar; therefore, the prototype is currently applicable only to Pantnagar’s agro-climatic conditions and requires local data and validation before use elsewhere.
  • Weather–pest relationships: Built on previously established research linking fall armyworm larval density with five meteorological parameters: minimum temperature, relative humidity, rainfall, sunshine hours, and wind velocity.
  • Statistical methods: Correlation and regression analysis of larval density against weather parameters.
  • Prediction engine: Implemented three prediction equations based on these established relationships, with automated logic to select or combine estimates according to their applicability.
  • Stage-specific IPM: Synthesised published research and pest-management guidelines to integrate four maize growth-stage windows with three larval-risk tiers, generating stage-specific management recommendations.
  • Pest-presence check: Since a phenology-based pest-arrival model is not integrated, moth-trap observations are required to confirm pest presence before the prototype estimates larval density.
How to Use the Prototype
  • Historical Data: Select a historical week to explore its weather inputs, moth-trap observations, estimated larval density, and corresponding IPM guidance.
  • New Data: Enter the meteorological week, weather parameters, and moth-trap observations for a new or upcoming week to generate an estimate and corresponding IPM recommendations, provided the required checks are satisfied.
Validation Check

Historical predictions were compared with recorded larval counts. Weeks with correct predictions are marked Validated, while weeks with incorrect predictions or those failing the model’s applicability checks are flagged Non-validated.

Key Achievements
  • 2024 Seasonal Performance: The prototype correctly predicted the observed larval density for every evaluated week (SMW 31–41) of the 2024 Kharif season at Pantnagar.
  • Historical Evaluation: Generated estimates for 26 of 49 historical weeks across 2021, 2022, and 2024; the remaining 23 weeks were flagged as non-validated.
  • Recognition: Selected as a finalist in a national hackathon.
